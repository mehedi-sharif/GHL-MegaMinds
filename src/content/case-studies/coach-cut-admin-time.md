<section><div class="wrap">
  <div class="sechead"><span class="label">The problem</span><h2>She was running<br>her own calendar by hand.</h2></div>
  <div class="grid g3">
    <div class="card hoverable"><span class="snum">P/01</span><h4>Booking was a conversation</h4><p>Every session took four or five messages back and forth to land on a time that worked. That is admin, not coaching.</p></div>
    <div class="card hoverable"><span class="snum">P/02</span><h4>People forgot they had booked</h4><p>No reminders went out, so about a third of sessions were no-shows. An empty hour still costs her the hour.</p></div>
    <div class="card hoverable"><span class="snum">P/03</span><h4>Chasing payment after the fact</h4><p>She invoiced after the session and then had to follow it up. Awkward for her, awkward for the client.</p></div>
  </div>
</div></section>
<section><div class="wrap">
  <div class="sechead"><span class="label">What we built</span><h2>Let the calendar<br>do the arguing.</h2>
  <p>Booking rules that match how she actually wants to work, then reminders and payment attached to them.</p></div>
  <div class="grid g2" style="gap:40px">
    <div class="card pad">
      <h3>The build</h3>
      <ul class="ticks">
        <li>Calendars with real rules: notice, buffers, daily limits</li>
        <li>Separate booking links for discovery and paid sessions</li>
        <li>Automatic reminders by text and email before each session</li>
        <li>Payment connected to booking, taken up front</li>
        <li>Follow up sequence for people who never rebooked</li>
        <li>One place to see the week ahead</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="card pad">
      <h3>How it ran</h3>
      <p>The rules were the hard part, not the software. We went through how she wants her week to look, then set the calendar so it refuses anything that breaks it.</p>
      <p style="margin-top:14px">Payment moving to the front of the session is what made the no-show number fall. People show up for things they have already paid for.</p>
