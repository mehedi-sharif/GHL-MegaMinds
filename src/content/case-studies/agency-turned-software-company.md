<section><div class="wrap">
  <div class="sechead"><span class="label">The problem</span><h2>He was selling software<br>one phone call at a time.</h2></div>
  <div class="grid g3">
    <div class="card hoverable"><span class="snum">P/01</span><h4>Every sign up needed him</h4><p>A new client meant a call, a manual account, and an invoice he raised himself. Four clients was manageable. Forty would not have been.</p></div>
    <div class="card hoverable"><span class="snum">P/02</span><h4>No plans, just prices he made up</h4><p>Each deal was priced on the call. Nothing repeatable, nothing he could put on a page and point people at.</p></div>
    <div class="card hoverable"><span class="snum">P/03</span><h4>Billing lived in his inbox</h4><p>Payments were chased by hand every month, which is fine until it is thirty of them.</p></div>
  </div>
</div></section>
<section><div class="wrap">
  <div class="sechead"><span class="label">What we built</span><h2>A product people<br>can buy on their own.</h2>
  <p>SaaS Mode turned on properly, with the plumbing that makes it a business rather than a setting.</p></div>
  <div class="grid g2" style="gap:40px">
    <div class="card pad">
      <h3>The build</h3>
      <ul class="ticks">
        <li>SaaS Mode switched on, configured, and tested end to end</li>
        <li>Stripe connected with rebilling that reconciles</li>
        <li>Three plans with real limits between them</li>
        <li>Self serve sign up and checkout page on his domain</li>
        <li>Snapshot loaded automatically for every new account</li>
        <li>Onboarding emails so day one is not silence</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="card pad">
      <h3>How it ran</h3>
      <p>The plans came first. Three tiers that are genuinely different, so people can pick without a call, and so upgrading means something.</p>
      <p style="margin-top:14px">After that it was wiring: checkout to account creation, account creation to snapshot, snapshot to onboarding email. He stopped being a step in his own sign up flow.</p>
